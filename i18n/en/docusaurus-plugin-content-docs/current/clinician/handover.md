# Handing a case over

A case belongs to one clinician at a time: the one who can edit it. Handing
it over passes it to a colleague, for example when the anaesthetist who saw
the patient the day before is not the one in theatre.

## Handing over

Press **Hand over** in the case's row on the dashboard (on the phone: hold the
case, then **Hand over**). Choose the colleague from **Select colleague**;
**Search…** narrows the list. Only colleagues in your own hospital are listed.

![Choosing a colleague](/img/screens/en/web-dashboard-handover.png)

**Send request** sends it. Until the colleague answers, the case stays yours
and shows **Awaiting acceptance**. **Withdraw** takes the request back.

## Receiving a case

A case offered to you appears in a yellow **Pending case handovers** banner
at the top of the dashboard, and under the **Handovers** scope.

- **Accept** makes it yours: you can now edit it.
- **Decline** leaves it with the colleague who sent it.

## Heads of department: Assign to

A head of department (and an administrator) sees **Assign to** instead of
**Hand over**. **Assign now** moves the case at once, without waiting for the
colleague to accept. Use it to distribute the day's cases. See
[Assigning cases](../head-of-department/assigning.md).

## What moves, and what does not

- The colleague can edit the case from then on. You keep read and print
  access, in the same hospital.
- The case stays in the hospital where the operation happens.
- **The case code may change.** Case codes are each clinician's own sequence
  (`2026-0001`, `2026-0002`, …). If the colleague already has a case with the
  same code, the case takes the next code in their sequence. The old code
  stays in the **Handover history** ("previously 2026-0438"), so the paper
  record can still be matched.

## When a case cannot be handed over

A finished case and a cancelled case cannot be handed over: there is nothing
left to do on them, so the button is hidden. A case can be offered to only one
colleague at a time.

## Handover history

The case's summary lists every handover: who offered it to whom, and whether
it was accepted, declined or withdrawn.

## In the preop summary

If the hospital uses the [preop summary](./preop-summary.md), it has its own
**Hand over** button, for passing the case to the colleague who will give the
anaesthetic.
